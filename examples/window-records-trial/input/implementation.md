# Implementation notebook

All records in this bundle are constructed teaching data, not a benchmark, field deployment, or genuine research result. No citations to outside research are claimed.

- The earlier collector already groups samples into 20 s event-time windows and computes a count and sum. Its ordinary late-data mode waits 120 s after a window closes, then emits one record. We did not invent event-time windows, aggregation, watermarks, deduplication, database transactions or version numbers. There is no systematic literature comparison.
- Every emitted record is (stream_id, window_end, revision, count, sum). Readers calculate the mean from count and sum. Each accepted revision replaces the entire previous record for that window. It is not an increment to the previous sum. The receiver accepts a record only when its revision is greater than the stored revision, updating count and sum together in one transaction. The transport may duplicate or reorder emitted records.
- Our input packet has a stable (stream_id, sample_id) and event timestamp. The prototype keeps seen sample IDs with each open or retained window. A repeated sample_id is ignored. This is a different duplicate from a repeated delivery of an already emitted aggregate record.
- Variant F120 emits once after waiting 120 s. Variant I5 emits once after waiting 5 s and never corrects that output. Variant R5 emits after 5 s and can replace the record during the retention interval. R5 retains window state until 120 s after window closure, independent of arrival time; it then evicts the state. Later packets are logged and rejected, never silently added to a new window. The closure and emission rule is the same event-time rule in all variants.
- A diagnostic U5 uses R5's generation side but removes the receiver's revision-order check. It still replaces a complete pair; it does not add deltas. Only the bursty trace was run for this diagnostic.
- The 48 windows in each profile are different windows, not 48 repeated runs of the same workload. One replay was constructed for each listed variant/profile. There are no timing confidence intervals or error bars. First-output wait is the configured wait, not a measured compute latency. Final agreement compares count and sum with a complete offline packet list, after the configured replay has finished.
- The bursty trace contains late arrivals reaching at most 90 s after closure. Seventeen windows need corrections. Duplicate and out-of-order aggregate deliveries are present. A stale revision overwrites a newer one in ten U5 windows. This diagnostic checks revision ordering under these deliveries, not every aspect of the system.
- The beyond-retention trace has twelve windows that are incomplete at 5 s. Eight are completed before 120 s and four receive a needed packet at 180 s. Those four cannot be repaired by either F120 or R5 under the fixed retention rule. No variant or threshold was re-tuned after looking at this profile.
- An early development notebook tried adding correction deltas at the receiver. Duplicate deliveries would count a correction twice. This was a reasoning failure used to choose whole-record replacement; no measured results from that early notebook are included.
- Peak retained-state values below count the serialized aggregate and sample-ID state in the constructed replay. They are not total process memory. R5 also incurs extra record transmissions; the table lists generated revisions after first emission, before network duplication. The U5 state and output counts match the corresponding R5 generation side.
- A reconnect test, multi-writer concurrency test, persistence failure test and long-running deployment were not performed. No claim of complete exactly-once processing is supported.

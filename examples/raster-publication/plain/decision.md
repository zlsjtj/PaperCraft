# Framing decision

**Condition:** ordinary task prompt; only the five supplied DEMO sources were read. No skills, literature search, outside feedback, or author approval were used.

Three routes were considered:

1. **A comprehensive visualization framework.** This follows the original title, but module count does not explain a contribution. It also obscures that immutable roots, publication, checksums, and dirty-tile tracking already existed.
2. **A new consistency mechanism that beats immediate tile transfer.** The two-tile example supports an accessible explanation, but FS already satisfies the coherence requirement. Centering the argument on IT would sidestep the stronger comparison and overstate what is new.
3. **Sparse transfer under an existing coherent-publication contract.** This makes the implementation work specific: acknowledged-base reuse, target-value coalescing, and validated publication under interrupted delivery. It also makes FS the relevant performance comparison and gives dense/local reversals a central role.

The final manuscript selects route 3. Its claim is deliberately limited to the supplied design and synthetic comparison. No literature survey supports novelty, and authored fixtures cannot support real-world performance. The title describes the contract and transfer problem without inventing a new acronym.

The one self-revision retained the first draft's route while making the three protocol obligations more explicit and bringing the negative cases closer to the positive results. This is an editorial selection, not human acceptance.

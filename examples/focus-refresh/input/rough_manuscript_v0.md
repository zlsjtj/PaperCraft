# DEMO — unfinished author draft, v0

Working title: **Calibration-free exchange of imaging carriers by reusing row focus information**

## Notes for an opening, not yet reconciled

An automated imaging station may examine several specimens held in a removable carrier. The operator exchanges a carrier when the imaging sequence is complete, after which the instrument must recover its coordinate system and focus settings. A factory calibration is attractive because repeated measurements consume time. The problem is that repeatable seating of the whole carrier and repeatable height of every specimen location are different properties. The present exercise uses a constructed nine-position carrier to study what happens when those properties are conflated. All results are DEMO calculations. We have not built an instrument or obtained customer observations.

Our original idea was that three measurements, one in the middle of each row, would make exchange effectively calibration-free. If a row does not move, its three old settings can remain in use. If it moves, the instrument can revisit only that row. This seems especially attractive when several specimen positions are unchanged. **Draft assertion to check: after the ordinary registration step, three row checks guarantee correct focus at every specimen location for all carrier deformations considered here.** The draft has not yet reconciled that assertion with the complete field records. Calling this procedure calibration-free is also provisional because measurements remain in the sequence.

The carrier is a rectangular plate with nine specimen apertures. The imaging head is represented by a square protective shoe around the optical axis. A narrow fixed bridge clip lies in the gap between the middle and northern rows. It does not cover any acquisition center. A direct move between two centers can nevertheless bring the shoe into the clip. The controller raises the head for such a segment and lowers it at the endpoint. This adds a route-dependent time cost even when both endpoints are valid imaging locations. Counting only measured specimen positions therefore seems incomplete, although the first version of our notes did exactly that.

## Apparatus fragments

The plate dimensions are 96 mm in x and 76 mm in y. The origin is the plate center, with east as positive x and north as positive y. The specimen centers occur at x = -32, 0, and 32 mm and y = -24, 0, and 24 mm. Their names combine a row letter S, M, or N and a column letter W, C, or E. Each aperture is 18 by 14 mm; an 8 by 6 mm imaging field is centered in it. This packet models the best-focus height at the center of that field, not the focus variation across its entire area. The specimen surface is treated as opaque to the height calculation; no optical scattering or image formation model has been included.

Two lateral fiducials on the carrier and three exposed datum tabs support a common registration operation. For this exercise the two-fiducial rigid xy registration and the datum-plane recovery are exact. Their operations take a prescribed total of 5.0 s. This is a deliberately favorable simplification, and it applies equally to all policies. The lateral transforms and plane coefficients still vary between events and are recorded so that the distinction between the global transform and local residual height is explicit. An old local residual map is stored at all nine centers. That map is not replaced by a plane.

The bridge clip occupies x = -10 to 10 mm and y = 9 to 15 mm, with its top at z = 3.4 mm in the dock frame. At the nominal low travel height the shoe underside is at z = 1.2 mm. The shoe extends 6 mm on each side of its optical center in both lateral axes. Center trajectories intersecting the resulting forbidden rectangle require a raised transfer. The raised underside is at z = 4.4 mm. The timing model charges 0.74 s for the complete raise, settle, lower, and settle operation associated with one intersecting segment. These values describe a constructed kinematic budget, not certified collision protection.

## Implementation notes that belong somewhere in methods

The policy named PLANE performs only the common registration and then uses the old nine-center residual map. Its name refers to the newly fitted global plane and does not imply that the old local map is zero. ALL9 performs the common registration and then measures every center. It follows a fixed serpentine ordering. ROW3 proceeds from the southern row to the middle row and then the northern row. It first measures the center-column position in the current row. If the magnitude of the difference from that position's old residual exceeds 8 micrometers, it measures the western and eastern positions immediately and replaces all three residual settings in that row. Otherwise it leaves the entire row's stored settings unchanged. The center measurement is reused when an update is triggered; it is not measured twice.

SHIFT3 also measures the three center-column positions, but it adds each observed center difference to all three old residual values in that row. It does not test a threshold and does not measure either outer position. It is an inexpensive alternative that assumes a common row displacement. Our earliest lab notes called every row operation an update; the actual code distinguishes a measured value, a trigger, and the value that is eventually commanded. Those distinctions matter when interpreting untriggered ROW3 rows and outer positions in SHIFT3.

All policies are applied independently to the same stored starting map for each event. They do not inherit an updated map from another policy or from the preceding event. The policies receive the same preconstructed reading if they measure the same center in the same event. A reader should not interpret the 96 policy runs as 96 independent exchanges. There are 24 constructed exchanges, each evaluated under four policies. There is no probability distribution over future carriers and no random sample of hardware units.

The old map is a shallow quadratic residual bowl. After exchange, a new global plane is added, along with a changed row residual, a small site texture, and in selected events an extra displacement at one eastern position. Local probe error is bounded in this construction and fixed by the event and site indices. Current probe readings include that error. The acceptance rule is an absolute residual focus error no greater than 8 micrometers at a center. An event is accepted only if all nine centers satisfy that rule. The phrase acceptable image in the early planning notes should be replaced or qualified because no image-quality measurement exists.

## Unsorted evaluation notes

We made eight broad-row cases, eight eastern-local cases, and eight combined cases. The broad-row cases include no large changes, one changed row, two changed rows, and three changed rows. The eastern-local cases place an extra offset at one eastern specimen position while keeping the center-column checks small. The combined cases place both a large row change and an eastern-local offset. In the first four combined cases the local offset is on the changed row. In the last four it is on another row. These labels describe how the cases were constructed. They are not a learned classifier or information available to the controller.

The complete data should be inspected before choosing the wording of the results. In particular, the policies differ in the number of accepted events as well as time. An average time over every attempt is straightforward to calculate but does not make failed attempts useful acquisitions. We did not construct a recovery policy after a failed attempt. There is no automatic retry, quality inspection cost, repair technician, or fallback-to-ALL9 cost hidden in the total. Adding one would require a new declared model. Dropping failed events from the comparison would change its meaning and must not happen silently.

The ALL9 schedule uses nine probes on every event, including the quiet case. A fixed schedule is easy to implement and covers all measured locations. ROW3 can use three, five, seven, or nine probes, depending on which rows trigger. The direct path between successive selected positions can be longer than expected. A middle-column check followed by a northern middle-column check passes the bridge; a row's outer points and the return to the park position can change both travel distance and raised-transfer count. We retain operation-by-operation traces rather than treating these differences as negligible.

The local probe stop is assigned 0.75 s, which includes its focus measurement and local settling. Lateral motion is represented at 80 mm/s with zero acceleration time. Each policy starts its local calibration at the park point, returns there after its local calibration, and then executes the same acquisition route. A calibration with no local measurements contributes zero local route distance. Acquisition uses nine 0.4 s stops and the same serpentine center ordering as ALL9. The acquisition route includes the return to park. Common registration, local calibration, and common acquisition must all be included when calling a number total cycle time.

## Loose discussion material

A practical instrument would need to establish the spatial correlation of focus changes using actual carriers. A row may move coherently after seating, but an inclusion under one specimen or a local change in a film could affect only one part of a row. The constructed cases exercise both possibilities without asserting how often they occur in a laboratory. The local height model is merely a set of nine points and does not describe a complete elastic plate. Its values therefore cannot be used to estimate stress, material modulus, contact pressure, or deformations between sample points.

We have also not measured how the autofocus routine behaves on low-texture specimens, how thermal drift progresses during a scan, or whether repeated raising introduces hysteresis. A real instrument would need to validate the datum measurement, lateral localization, safe trajectory generation, and optical focus metric together. Here the datum fit is exact, measurements do not damage specimens, and timing does not vary between events except through the declared route and probe count. These assumptions are useful for checking the bookkeeping but are not empirical assurances.

The draft needs a conclusion that agrees with both the unsuccessful and successful constructed cases. It should distinguish a reusable mapping idea from evidence that the mapping will remain valid after an arbitrary exchange. A physically persuasive claim would require a defined carrier family, a measured deformation envelope, repeated seating trials, and image-level acceptance. A literature comparison is also still missing. No statement about priority, novelty, superiority over published methods, or readiness for unattended operation is supported by this packet alone.
